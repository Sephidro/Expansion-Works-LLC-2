Guide 01 · Speed to lead

How Fast Should a Consulting Firm Respond to New Inquiries?

A faster useful response keeps more consulting inquiries in the conversation.

By Xavier Pearson · Updated August 15, 2026 · 6 min read

When a lead goes quiet after inquiring, they may still be interested. Often, they have already started talking to someone who answered sooner.

Someone fills out a form, sends a message, asks a question, and then stops talking. It is easy to read that as low interest. But buyers often contact two or three providers at once, then continue with the first one who gives them a useful response.

What happens while the inquiry waits

An inquiry lands in an inbox, a DM, or a form notification while you are working on something else. It may sit for an hour or until tomorrow. Meanwhile, the buyer keeps comparing options and may already be talking to whoever replied first.

The first useful response gets the buyer's attention and starts a real conversation. By the time you reply, another provider may already understand the problem, answer the first questions, and schedule the next step.

A follow-up gap I fixed

While running growth for a NYC charter school, I found 207 applications that had been started but not completed. These families had already shown interest. The process needed a clear acknowledgment, reminders when they stalled, and human follow-up for anyone who still needed help.

The school had 207 unfinished next steps sitting inside demand it had already earned.

We acknowledged the application, sent structured reminders, kept unfinished applications visible, and assigned human follow-up. 105 of the 207 later reached completion. Across two recorded years, enrolled students associated with that recovered flow represented approximately $1.7M in public funding value.

A consulting inquiry or discovery call is different from a school application, but the checkpoint is familiar: someone showed real interest, the next step stayed unfinished, and the business needed to keep that person visible long enough to follow up.

Make the response work while you are busy

Checking your phone more often works only until delivery, meetings, or the rest of the business takes over. Some days you see the inquiry immediately. Other days it sits for six hours.

Send an immediate acknowledgment with a clear response time, then put the human follow-up on a schedule. The buyer knows what happens next, and your response no longer depends on noticing the inquiry in real time.

None of this requires more leads. It requires catching the ones you already have before they decide the silence is your answer.
