Consulting Inquiry Follow-Up Emails: What to Send and When

Answer the buyer's real question and give them a clear next step while the inquiry is still fresh.

By Xavier Pearson · Updated August 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Most follow-up emails read like a form because they were written to sound “professional.” Write the email the way you would explain the answer to a person standing in front of you. Help them make the next decision.

Answer the question they asked

If someone asks “does this work for my situation,” answer that question and explain when it may not fit. A specific answer gives the buyer something useful to respond to. A feature list makes them sort through the decision alone.

Ask a question they can answer

A question invites a response. A statement invites silence. “Here's why we're a great fit” leaves nothing to answer. “What happens now when a lead goes quiet?” gives the prospect an easy place to continue the conversation and tells you what to address next.

Write the way you speak when you are genuinely trying to help someone decide.

Why Speed Still Wins Even With The Best Copy In The World

None of this matters if it arrives two days later. As the response-time guide explains, the first useful response often starts the conversation while the buyer is still thinking about the problem. Three days later, even a great email may arrive after they have begun deciding with someone else.

Use AI for speed, then make the judgment yourself

I use AI to draft faster and catch generic language before it goes out. Then I make the judgment myself. Read each paragraph and ask whether it could be sent to any lead. If it could, add the real situation, question, or next step that belongs to this person.
