Simple Lead Source Tracking for Consulting Firms

Track where the last five clients came from and know which channel deserves more attention.

By Xavier Pearson · Updated August 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Review the last five clients and the source field is often blank. You may remember LinkedIn, a referral, or word of mouth, but the answer is hard to trust when nobody recorded it at the time.

Tracking the source requires one question at the moment the lead shows up. That moment gets skipped because you are busy answering what they asked and moving the conversation forward.

The Blur, And Why It Happens

A lead may see a LinkedIn post, hear your name from a past client, and fill out a form weeks later. Ask how they heard about you and they may name only the most recent touch. Record the answer consistently, and use distinct links when you need to separate first touch from the source that finally produced the inquiry.

Most intake forms and DMs do not ask the source question, or they ask it differently each time. One answer lives in a half-remembered phone call. Another disappears when the conversation moves straight to scheduling. A consistent question creates something you can count.

Why source data changes where you spend time

Without source data, the loudest channel gets the most attention. Content looks like work, while a referral partner may quietly send half the business and receive only a thank-you text. Measure each source so you can spend time on the channels that produce clients.

What To Instrument First

Start with three habits. You do not need a dashboard or a $200-a-month attribution platform yet.

One consistent question, asked the same way every time. “How'd you hear about us?” on the intake form, on the first call, or in the first DM reply. Ask it the same way every time so the answers are comparable.

Distinct links if you're running more than one channel. If you post on LinkedIn and also run a referral program and also do local networking, a different link (or even just a different landing page) for each one turns "I think it was LinkedIn" into "the link tagged LinkedIn got clicked, and this lead came through it."

Write the source down when the lead converts. A spreadsheet with one row per client and a source column is enough. Record it while the answer is fresh instead of reconstructing six months later when revenue dips.

Collect enough evidence to name where the business comes from and which channels deserve more attention.

The Trap: Don't Over-Build This

Run the one-question habit by hand for a month before buying software. For many small service businesses, asking and recording that question consistently produces enough information. If the spreadsheet becomes hard to maintain, you will know exactly what the next tool needs to do.

Find the first place your lead process needs work

Nine questions. About three minutes. Your result appears immediately.
