Where Are Your Leads Actually Coming From

If you can't answer this about your last five clients, you're marketing by vibes.

5 min read · Lead source tracking

Ask most service business owners where their last five clients came from and you'll get a shrug dressed up as an answer. "Mostly LinkedIn, I think." "A lot of it's referrals." "Word of mouth, probably." Nobody's lying. They genuinely don't know, because nothing in their process ever forced the question.

This isn't a knock on anyone's business sense. Tracking where a lead came from requires asking at the exact moment they show up, and that moment usually gets skipped because you're busy answering the actual question they asked, not running an intake survey.

The Blur, And Why It Happens

A real lead's path is rarely one clean line. They saw a LinkedIn post three weeks ago, forgot about it, got referred by a past client last week, then finally filled out your form today because the referral reminded them you exist. Ask them "how'd you hear about us" and they'll probably say the referral, because that's what's top of mind, even though the post did the actual work of making your name familiar. Multiply that by every lead, and "mostly referrals" becomes a story you're telling yourself, not a measurement.

The other reason it's blurry: most intake forms and DMs don't ask the question at all, and if they do, it's not asked the same way every time. One lead gets asked in a phone call and you half-remember the answer. Another never gets asked because the conversation moved straight to scheduling. There's no consistent capture point, so there's nothing to actually count.

Why This Actually Costs You Money

Without a real answer, you can't tell the difference between a channel that's working and one that's just loud. Content takes visible effort, so it feels like it's doing something. A referral partner quietly sending you half your business might get a thank-you text and nothing else, because it doesn't feel like "marketing," it just feels like luck. You end up spending time on the channel that's satisfying to work on, not the one that's actually converting, because you've never measured which is which.

What To Instrument First

Not a dashboard. Not a $200-a-month attribution platform. Three habits, in order:

One consistent question, asked the same way every time. "How'd you hear about us?" on the intake form, on the first call, in the first DM reply, whatever your actual first-contact moment is. The value isn't the sophistication of the question, it's that it gets asked identically every single time so the answers are actually comparable.

Distinct links if you're running more than one channel. If you post on LinkedIn and also run a referral program and also do local networking, a different link (or even just a different landing page) for each one turns "I think it was LinkedIn" into "the link tagged LinkedIn got clicked, and this lead came through it."

A log that gets written at the moment of conversion, not reconstructed later. A spreadsheet with one row per client and a source column is enough. The discipline isn't the tool, it's writing it down when it happens instead of trying to remember six months from now when you're wondering why revenue dipped.

The goal isn't a perfect attribution model. It's being able to say, with actual evidence, "this is where my business comes from," instead of guessing and hoping the guess is close enough.

The Trap: Don't Over-Build This

The temptation once you notice the gap is to go buy software. Resist that until you've run the one-question habit for a month by hand. Most small service businesses don't have a volume problem that needs a platform, they have a discipline problem that needs one question asked consistently. Fix the habit first. If you outgrow a spreadsheet, that's a good problem, and it means you'll actually know what a fancier tool needs to do for you instead of guessing at that too.
