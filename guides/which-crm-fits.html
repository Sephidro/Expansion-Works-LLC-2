Which CRM Actually Fits Where You're At

HubSpot free tier, GoHighLevel, or a spreadsheet: pick based on where you are, not what's trendy.

5 min read · Tool selection

A lot of CRM decisions get made backwards. Someone sees a platform recommended in a course or a LinkedIn post, signs up, and spends the next month trying to justify the subscription instead of asking whether it matched what their business actually needed that week. You don't need the fanciest tool. You need the one that fits where you actually are right now.

If You're Not Making Consistent Revenue From This Yet: Start Free

HubSpot's free CRM tier is real, and it's been around long enough to trust: contact records, a deal pipeline, basic email tracking. It's not sophisticated. It's a spreadsheet with a nicer interface and a couple of built-in reminders. That's the point. At this stage, the job isn't sophistication, it's getting every lead into one place instead of scattered across your inbox, your texts, and your memory.

Also legitimate at this stage: an actual spreadsheet. One row per lead. Columns for source, status, last touch, next follow-up date (see the attribution guide and the follow-up guide for what those columns should actually track). The tool matters less than the discipline of updating it every single time. Don't buy a $300-a-month agency stack before you've proven you'll actually use a free one consistently. That's not the software's fault, it's a habit that has to exist first.

Once Follow-Up Is Real And You've Outgrown Manual Sending: GoHighLevel-Type Platforms

All-in-one platforms like GoHighLevel exist for a specific stage: pipeline, automated sequences, booking, and messaging all need to talk to each other, and you're now losing more to a fragmented stack of separate tools than you'd spend on one real platform. That's genuinely more than a solo operator or someone still testing an offer needs.

The tell you're ready isn't that the platform looks impressive in a demo. It's that you already have a follow-up sequence running (guide 03) and it's outgrown what you can send by hand.

Where Your Website Fits In This, Separately

Worth naming clearly, because people conflate these: your website and your CRM do two different jobs. The site's job is capturing the lead, meaning a form that actually reaches somewhere real. The CRM's job is tracking what happens after. Don't expect a website platform to double as your CRM, and don't buy CRM software to try to fix a website that isn't capturing leads in the first place. That second problem is an attribution and conversion problem, not a tooling problem.

The Actual Test, Regardless Of What You Pick

Can you list, right now, every open lead and what's supposed to happen next for each one? If yes, whatever you're using is working, free or paid. If no, the tool was never the real problem. The discipline of using one, consistently, is.
