CRM or Spreadsheet for Consultants: When to Switch

Choose HubSpot, GoHighLevel, or a spreadsheet based on your active opportunities, handoffs, and follow-up volume.

By Xavier Pearson · Updated August 15, 2026 · 5 min read

A course or LinkedIn post recommends a CRM, so someone signs up and spends the next month trying to make the business fit the software. Start with the work you need to track today, then choose the smallest tool that can handle it.

If You're Not Making Consistent Revenue From This Yet: Start Free

HubSpot's free CRM tier includes contact records, a deal pipeline, and basic email tracking. That is enough to get every lead out of scattered inboxes, texts, and memory and into one visible place.

A spreadsheet also works at this stage. Use one row per lead with columns for source, status, last touch, and next follow-up date (see the attribution guide and the follow-up guide). Update it every time. Prove that habit with the free tool before you pay for a larger stack.

Once Follow-Up Is Real And You've Outgrown Manual Sending: GoHighLevel-Type Platforms

All-in-one platforms like GoHighLevel exist for a specific stage: pipeline, automated sequences, booking, and messaging all need to talk to each other, and you're now losing more to a fragmented stack of separate tools than you'd spend on one real platform. That's genuinely more than a solo operator or someone still testing an offer needs.

Move up when a working follow-up sequence has outgrown what you can send and track by hand (guide 03).

Your website and CRM handle different steps

The website captures the inquiry and sends it somewhere dependable. The CRM tracks what happens after. Fix capture on the website. Use the CRM for ownership, next steps, follow-up, and outcomes.

The test for any CRM or spreadsheet

Can you list every open lead and what should happen next right now? If yes, keep using the current tool. If no, choose one place to track every opportunity and update it consistently.
