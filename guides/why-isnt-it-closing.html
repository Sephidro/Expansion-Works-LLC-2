Why More Traffic Won't Fix A Leaky Middle

If people are inquiring but not buying, the fix isn't more people inquiring.

5 min read · Qualification & close rate

Close rate feels low, so the instinct is to get more leads. More content, more ads, more networking, more of whatever's currently bringing people in. It feels productive because it's visible activity. It's also usually the wrong move, because if the actual conversation isn't converting the people you already have, more people just means more of the same conversation ending the same way.

A leaky bucket doesn't get fixed by pouring in faster. It gets fixed by finding the hole.

Where The Middle Actually Leaks

Three places, almost always. First: nobody's qualifying the lead before the actual sales conversation happens, so a chunk of every call or meeting is spent with someone who was never a real fit, whether that's budget, timeline, or the problem itself. Second: the pitch is generic, the same pitch for everyone regardless of what they actually said they needed, so it never quite lands as an answer to their specific problem. Third: there's no clear next step built into the conversation, so it just... ends, and "let me think about it" becomes the default outcome instead of an actual decision either way.

Qualifying Isn't Gatekeeping, It's Respect

A lot of business owners resist asking qualifying questions because it feels pushy, like they're screening people out. It's the opposite. Not qualifying means spending an hour with someone who was never going to buy, which wastes their time as much as yours. Asking two or three sharp questions early does two things at once: it filters out the conversations that were never going anywhere, and it hands you exactly what the person who is a fit actually cares about, so the pitch you give them is specific instead of generic.

A specific pitch to the right person closes more often than a great pitch to everyone.

What To Check Before Spending On More Traffic

Three questions, honestly answered. Does every inquiry get asked the same two or three qualifying questions before the pitch, or does the conversation just launch straight into a generic explanation of what you do? Does every conversation end with an explicit next step (a date, a decision, a follow-up scheduled) or does it just trail off? Is the actual pitch built from what the person said they needed, or is it the same script regardless of who's on the call?

If the honest answer to any of those is "no" or "it depends," that's the leak. Fix it before spending another dollar getting more people into the same conversation.
