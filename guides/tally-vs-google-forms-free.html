Tally Is the First Free Google Forms Upgrade I’d Test

Tally gives away enough to build a serious consultant intake. The real test is whether the lead lands somewhere useful after the form is submitted.

By Xavier Pearson · Updated September 26, 2026 · Facts checked September 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Tally gives away the exact features that usually send me looking for a pricing page: conditional logic, calculations, payments, file uploads, Google Sheets syncing, webhooks, and an AI connection that can build and edit forms through conversation.

That list is why I opened the tab. The handoff is what has to earn it a permanent place.

My verdict: Tally is the first free form builder I would test after Google Forms gets in the way. If Google Forms already collects enough information and somebody actually follows up, leave it alone. A prettier form is not an operating system.

What Tally is giving away

Tally says 99% of its features are available free under its fair-use policy, with unlimited forms and submissions for normal use. Pro is $29 per month. Business is $89. That means you can build a serious inquiry flow before the upgrade screen starts following you around with a clipboard.

The AI connection is the part I want to play with. Tally says its MCP server exposes 21 tools across form creation, editing, organization, and submission analysis. It works with compatible assistants including ChatGPT and Claude, costs nothing extra, and does not let the assistant delete forms or submissions.

That is a genuinely useful guardrail. It is also how people end up building a 34-question intake form because the robot made it easy. Faster form building does not fix bad questions.

The evidence boundary

I have not run Tally through a live client intake during this cycle. This verdict comes from Tally’s current documentation, checked on 2026-09-24. The feature list is verified. The setup time, failure points, notification behavior, and maintenance burden still need a hands-on test.

The first test I would run

I would build one fake consulting inquiry from end to end. Pick a service. Trigger a different follow-up question. Upload a dummy brief under 10 MB. Send the answers to Google Sheets. Fire a webhook. Then submit the kind of incomplete, awkward answer a real person would send at 11:47 p.m.

The test is not whether the form looks nice. I want to see whether the right row appears, the right person gets notified, the file opens for the right people, and someone can tell what to do next without asking who built the automation.

I would not put payments in the first test. Tally’s payment block works with Stripe on free and paid accounts, adds no fee beyond Stripe’s standard charges, and supports one-time payments. It does not support recurring payments, and Tally says Stripe test cards still cannot be used.

I am not turning the first experiment into a real charge because the sandbox did not make the guest list. Prove the intake and handoff first. Add payment when the job genuinely requires money before the next step.

The file-upload catch

Free accounts can collect files up to 10 MB each. Pro removes that limit. The sharper issue is access after export.

Tally says uploaded-file links sent to third-party tools include access tokens. If a submission lands in Google Sheets, anyone with access to that Sheet can open the file without signing in to Tally.

That may be fine for a logo, portfolio, or dummy brief. It is a different decision for tax records, identification, health information, or confidential client documents. Use a form for the job it can safely perform. Sensitive collection needs a process designed for sensitive data.

When Google Forms still wins

Keep Google Forms if you need a name, email address, a few useful questions, and a dependable row in a Sheet. Keep it if a real person checks the responses, replies on time, and records what happened next.

That setup is not primitive. It works.

Move when you can name the thing Google Forms is preventing you from doing. Maybe every service needs different questions. Maybe a designer needs files without forcing the respondent into a Google login. Maybe the form needs to route answers into an automation instead of becoming a spreadsheet-shaped waiting room.

If the answer is “nothing, really,” close the Tally tab. You just saved yourself a migration and one more password.

When the paid plans earn their keep

Tally Pro earns its $29 when you need a custom domain, no Tally branding, partial submissions, team workspaces, deeper customization, or files larger than 10 MB. Tally Business earns its $89 when email verification and automatic submission-retention controls solve a requirement you can name.

Do not buy either plan to make the form feel more official while the lead still sits unanswered. Branding does not follow up.

My current verdict

Tally is the first free form builder I would test after Google Forms genuinely gets in the way. The free plan has enough room to build a real qualification and routing flow without designing around an upgrade wall.

I would start with a fake inquiry, a dummy upload, a Sheet, and one webhook. I would deliberately send bad answers and break the handoff. I would keep payments out until the rest of the path works.

The form is only the front door. The win is knowing the right person came through it and what happens next.
