THE SHORT ANSWER

A consultant needs a CRM when the process stops being reliably visible.

Not when a vendor says the business is growing. Not when a peer posts a dashboard. The operational breakpoint is when one or more of these failures keeps repeating.

KEEP THE SPREADSHEET

One owner can see every open opportunity.

Each prospect has a stage, next action, and date. Follow-up rarely slips. A new platform would add upkeep before it adds control.

FIX THE PROCESS FIRST

You cannot name the stages or measure the leak.

Define what happens from first interest to paid. Software cannot repair an undefined process. It only makes the confusion more expensive.

INSTALL A CRM

Visibility fails under real demand.

Opportunities disappear, follow-up repeats late, or two people need the same context. A lightweight CRM can now remove a demonstrated failure.

CONNECT THE SYSTEM

The handoffs, not the database, are breaking.

Multiple intake sources, owners, and repeated workflows need one record and one next step. Buying a CRM alone will not connect them.

THE PART VENDOR GUIDES SKIP

A CRM does not create follow-through. It exposes whether follow-through exists.

If nobody owns the next action, the CRM becomes a cleaner place for deals to die. Define the behavior first: one owner, one stage, one next action, one date, and one recorded outcome. Then choose the smallest tool that makes that behavior easier to maintain.

Questions consultants ask before switching.

Do I need a CRM as a solo consultant?

Not automatically. A spreadsheet can remain the better system while you can see every active opportunity, follow-up rarely slips, and each prospect has a clear next action and date.

When is a spreadsheet no longer enough?

The breakpoint is operational, not aspirational. A CRM becomes justified when active opportunities are hard to scan, follow-up repeatedly slips, more than one person needs the same context, or leads disappear without a recorded outcome.

Should I fix the process before buying a CRM?

Yes. Define the stages, owner, next action, and response expectation first. A new platform cannot repair an undefined process.

A CRM is one decision. Your lead path is the system.
