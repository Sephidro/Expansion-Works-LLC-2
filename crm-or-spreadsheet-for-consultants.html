THE SHORT ANSWER

You need a CRM when you can no longer see or follow up with every active opportunity.

These four results show what to keep, what to fix, and when to switch.

KEEP THE SPREADSHEET

One owner can see every open opportunity.

Each prospect has a stage, next action, and date. Follow-up rarely slips. A new platform would add upkeep before it adds control.

FIX THE PROCESS FIRST

Write down the stages before choosing software.

List what happens from first interest to paid, who owns each step, and how long each step should take. Then choose a tool that supports it.

INSTALL A CRM

Active opportunities are getting hard to see.

Opportunities disappear, follow-up runs late, or two people need the same context. A lightweight CRM can keep the work visible.

CONNECT THE SYSTEM

Several people and tools now share the same opportunity.

Connect the intake sources, owners, and repeated workflows around one record and one next step.

FOLLOW-UP STILL NEEDS AN OWNER

A CRM does not guarantee follow-up gets done.

Give every active opportunity one owner, one stage, one next action, one date, and one recorded outcome. Then choose the smallest tool that helps your team keep doing it.

Questions consultants ask before switching.

Do I need a CRM as a solo consultant?

Use a spreadsheet while you can see every active opportunity, follow-up rarely slips, and each prospect has a clear next action and date.

When is a spreadsheet no longer enough?

Move to a CRM when active opportunities are hard to scan, follow-up repeatedly slips, more than one person needs the same context, or leads disappear without a recorded outcome.

Should I fix the process before buying a CRM?

Yes. Define the stages, owner, next action, and response expectation first. A new platform cannot repair an undefined process.
