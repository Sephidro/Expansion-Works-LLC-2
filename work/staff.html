CASE 02 // STAFF APPRECIATION REDESIGN RESULT: STAFF SAVED AND SHARED THE FLYER

A Staff Appreciation Schedule
People Kept.

The school already had the week planned: bagels, a raffle, and a shoutout board. I redesigned the schedule so the presentation matched the care behind those plans. Staff left copies on their desks, framed one in a classroom, and asked for other flyers to receive the same treatment.

What the first flyer communicated

When you hand someone a clip-art Word document that says “You’re Valued,” the rushed layout undercuts the words before they finish reading them.

Companies give external marketing careful design, then print internal communication on whatever fits the breakroom printer. Staff see the difference.

The Fix: Treat Internal Like External

I told the AI to avoid corporate clip art, primary colors, and dense walls of text. I wanted a calm, editorial page that someone might save.

// The art direction I gave the AI const styleGuide = { vibe: "Calm editorial wellness with a premium feel", banned: ["clip art", "primary colors", "dense text blocks"], palette: { base: "Warm cream", text: "Deep charcoal", accents: "Muted sage and soft terracotta" }, type: "Elegant serif headlines, clean sans-serif body", spacing: "Let it breathe. Lots of white space." };

The design needed to match the care of the gesture. A message that says “you matter” should look like someone spent time on it.

Same Week, Different Feeling

The week itself was unchanged. Bagels Monday. Raffle Wednesday. Shoutout board Friday. The gifts and budget stayed the same.

Staff talked about the schedule. People asked who made it. Someone framed a copy in a classroom. Another committee asked me to redesign its flyer “like that one.”

Why It Worked

The schedule made the whole week feel more intentional because it looked like somebody thought the staff were worth the effort.

  • The old way: Cluttered, generic, "internal use only" energy. Reads as obligation.
  • The new way: Editorial design. Reads as deliberate care.

What the redesign changed

Staff notice when internal communication receives the same care as external marketing. That care makes the message feel deliberate.

Your internal documents show how much care you put into the people who already work there.

Every email, page, and customer touchpoint tells people how seriously you take them. That feeling affects what they do next.
