CASE 02 // STAFF APPRECIATION REDESIGN RESULT: STAFF ACTUALLY KEPT THE FLYER

Making the Schedule
The Actual Gift.

Every school does a Staff Appreciation Week. They print the schedule on regular printer paper, slap some clip art on it, and slide it into mailboxes. It says "we appreciate you" in Comic Sans and the staff sees right through it. I made a different schedule. Same bagels. Same raffle. Same shoutouts. Different schedule. Staff kept theirs on their desks.

What a Cheap Flyer Actually Says

Here's the thing nobody wants to admit. When you hand someone a clip-art Word doc that says "You're Valued!", what they hear is "you're an obligation we're checking off." The flyer is the message. The flyer was the message before they even read the words.

Companies spend real money on their external marketing. Gradients. Custom fonts. Photo shoots. The whole thing. Then internal comms get whatever fits on a piece of copy paper printed in the breakroom. Your staff sees both. They notice.

The Fix: Treat Internal Like External

I told the AI exactly what I didn't want. No corporate clip art. No primary colors. No dense walls of text. I wanted it to feel like a menu at a nice spa or a page in a magazine you'd actually save.

// The art direction I gave the AI const styleGuide = { vibe: "Editorial wellness. Calm. Premium.", banned: ["clip art", "primary colors", "dense text blocks"], palette: { base: "Warm cream", text: "Deep charcoal", accents: "Muted sage and soft terracotta" }, type: "Elegant serif headlines, clean sans-serif body", spacing: "Let it breathe. Lots of white space." };

The point wasn't to be fancy. The point was to match the care of the gesture to the look of the gesture. You can't say "you matter" on a piece of paper that says "you're an afterthought."

Same Week, Different Feeling

The week itself was unchanged. Bagels Monday. Raffle Wednesday. Shoutout board Friday. None of the actual gifts changed. None of the budget changed.

What changed was how it landed. Staff actually talked about it. People asked who made it. Someone framed theirs in their classroom. Someone from a different committee asked if I could redo their flyer "like that one."

Why It Worked

It wasn't the bagels that made them feel valued. It was the fact that the schedule looked like somebody thought they were worth the effort. The packaging IS the message. People know.

  • The old way: Cluttered, generic, "internal use only" energy. Reads as obligation.
  • The new way: Editorial design. Reads as deliberate care.

The Real Lesson

If you treat your staff like external customers, with the same design care and the same intentionality, they notice immediately. Most organizations don't. That gap is the opportunity.

Your internal documents tell people what you actually think of them. The "for internal use only" stamp is everywhere, even when you don't print it on the page.

This is the same thing I do for businesses. Every touchpoint, every email, every page on your site. The design isn't about looking good. It's about what it makes people feel about you. That's the part that drives the behavior you actually want.
