LEAD RECOVERY CASE // NYC CHARTER SCHOOL ENROLLMENT

207 people raised their hands. 105 finished after the follow-up system.

This is the operating case behind Expansion Works. It is not consulting-firm proof and it is not a promise of recovered revenue. It is evidence that a raised-hand-to-completion failure can be found, made visible, and systematically worked.

207started, then abandoned
105later completed
~70remaining warm leads
~$1.7Massociated funding estimate across two recorded years

THE FAILURE

Interest existed. Completion did not.

Prospective families had already started an application. That meant the school did not need to manufacture awareness for this group. The open loop was between “I am interested” and “I finished the next required step.”

Before the recovery process, abandoned applications were easy to treat as dead. In reality, many were delayed, confused, distracted, or waiting for a useful response. The first operating decision was to keep those people visible long enough for a human to act.

THE MECHANISM

One open loop. One visible queue. Multiple chances to finish.

The value did not come from a more impressive tool. It came from making the unfinished state operational.

01

Identify people who started the application but did not complete it.

Track completion and enrollment separately so activity is not mistaken for an outcome.

ATTRIBUTION LIMITS

What this case does not prove.

  • It does not prove the same completion rate or economic result in a consulting firm.
  • It was not a randomized experiment. Reminders, human follow-up, timing, school demand, and family circumstances can all affect completion.
  • Application completion is not the same as enrollment. Those outcomes must be tracked separately.
  • The funding number is an associated-value estimate across two recorded years, not cash collected by Expansion Works and not a recovery guarantee.
  • The mechanism only works when someone owns the exceptions that automation cannot resolve.

WHY IT TRANSFERS

The industry changes. The open loop does not.

A consulting inquiry, booked call, or sent proposal is not an enrollment application. The commercial stakes, timing, and buyer behavior differ. The transferable mechanism is narrower: a person has shown real intent, the next step is incomplete, and the business needs to know who owns that open loop.

That is the failure Expansion Works looks for in the last 10 serious inquiries. Not “how do we automate everything?” First: where did a good-fit buyer stop moving, was the next action visible, and did anyone own it?
