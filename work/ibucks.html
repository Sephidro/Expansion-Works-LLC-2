Why the Old System Failed

Schools love to buy software. They see a problem (student behavior), find a vendor, sign a check ($10K), put up a poster, and call it done.

Three months in, most staff had stopped using it. Students did not care about the points or the same generic rewards every school offered. The school kept paying while participation fell.

The old program treated behavior like a vending machine. Drop in a reward, get out a compliant student. Teenagers did not care about generic points or prizes, so the software could not create the behavior the school wanted.

What Wasn't Working
  • A few staff used it daily. Most didn't bother. No accountability, no buy-in.
  • Students had little reason to check or save their balance because the points bought generic prizes they did not want.
  • Rewards were generic. Same pencils, same erasers, same "fun day" passes every other school hands out.
  • The program cost $10,000 every year and had no way to fund the next round of rewards.

Build a currency students would care about

I replaced the platform with a school currency called iBucks.

Students earned iBucks for attendance, behavior, academic goals, and contributions to the school. They spent it at WarriorWear, the student-run store, on hoodies, hats, and other merchandise they wanted.

A generic point total was easy to forget. A visible balance that students could save and spend on something they wanted gave the number weight.

The Old Way

Staff awarded points that students traded for generic prizes. The balance carried little status or reason to save, while the platform cost $10,000 a year.

The iBucks Way

Students earned, saved, and spent iBucks across a catalog of merchandise they wanted. The balance carried status and gave them a reason to keep participating.

How the Loop Works

The system had three connected parts.

  • Earn: Staff give iBucks for attendance, behavior, academic wins, and helping out. Takes under 30 seconds. Any staff member can do it.
  • Spend: Students trade iBucks at WarriorWear, the student-run school store, for merchandise priced from $25 to $200.
  • Fund: WarriorWear profits pay for the next round of merchandise students buy with iBucks.

We replaced a $10K subscription with a system that paid for its own rewards, taught students to run a real business, and contributed an estimated $100K to $200K a year in new enrollment funding.

Student referrals became a second return

Students with the biggest iBucks balances became walking advertisements for the school. A large balance carried visible status, and the students most invested in earning it were also some of the students most engaged with the school.

I made the referral behavior official. A student received a large iBucks bonus after a referred transfer student enrolled. The new student received a starter bonus after the first month so they could buy something and feel included.

The Math on Referrals

The students most engaged with iBucks also knew other students who might fit the school. The referral bonus gave them a concrete reason to make the introduction.

  • The referring student received a large iBucks bonus after enrollment was confirmed.
  • The new student received a starter balance after 30 days and could buy something from the school store.
  • In NYC, each enrolled student is worth about $20K a year in per-pupil funding. One successful referral covered the entire annual program budget.
  • The referred students arrived with a trusted connection to someone already enrolled.

One student referred all five of her closest friends. Every one of them enrolled. Every one of them became a strong student. The referral mechanic didn't just fill seats. It shaped the entire incoming cohort.

Why staff adoption reached 95%

Internal tools lose staff when a simple action takes five clicks, a separate login, another app, and training people forget by Friday.

The Design That Made Adoption Easy

Awarding a student took under 30 seconds from the Google Sheet staff already used. One walkthrough covered the process.

  • Staff awarded iBucks from a Google Sheet they already had open.
  • Preset categories turned the entry into one quick selection.
  • Balances updated in real time. Students saw the change immediately and asked staff to keep using it.

The system reached 95% staff adoption because awarding iBucks took under 30 seconds inside tools staff already used.

A new principal came in and said she didn't believe in token economies. She canceled it without looking at the numbers. Enrollment dropped the next year. By then, the system supported behavior, the school store, referrals, and enrollment.

What made the system last five years

Over five years, iBucks grew from a behavior program into a school currency that supported retention, referrals, the student store, and enrollment.

The Five Things That Made It Work
  • Give the points something students want to save and spend. A visible balance, useful merchandise, and social status kept the currency relevant after class ended.
  • Connect earning with something worth buying. WarriorWear revenue helped pay for the next round of rewards.
  • Ask engaged students for referrals. Their referrals brought in other students who already had a trusted connection to the school.
  • Make the action fit the tools people already use. 95% of staff used iBucks because it added almost no new effort.
  • Document the operating and financial results. The next leader needs to see what the program supports before changing it.

I use the same four checks in business systems: make the action easy, give people a reason to keep using it, connect the cost to a useful return, and track the result. Then I fix the first place that stops working.
