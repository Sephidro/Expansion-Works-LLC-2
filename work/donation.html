CASE 01 // DONATION PAGE REBUILD RESULT: MORE DONATIONS, SAME TRAFFIC

A Pretty Page
That Didn't Make Money.

The page looked incredible. Dark, sharp, brutalist, every designer's wet dream. It also wasn't bringing in donations. Beautiful and broken aren't mutually exclusive. I rebuilt three things. The pricing tiers. The copy. And what actually happens the second somebody clicks "Donate." Here's exactly what changed and why it worked.

The $50 to $250 Cliff

Their donation page had two options. Fifty bucks or two hundred fifty. Nothing in between. That's a 5x jump with no place to land.

If you're staring at that grid and you actually want to give something meaningful, your brain does this thing. Fifty feels too small if you're being serious. Two-fifty feels like a lot. So you don't pick either. You close the tab. You tell yourself you'll come back. You don't come back.

That's not the donor being cheap. That's the page making them work too hard to decide.

The Fix: A $100 Bridge

I added a $100 tier. Not because I expected people to pick $100 (though a bunch did). I added it so $250 stopped feeling like a leap and started feeling like a step up.

  • $50: Buys the materials a student actually uses.
  • $100: Funds a local project from start to finish.
  • $250: Changes the trajectory of one kid's year.

Same page, one new button. Donations went up across every tier. The middle option pulled people up the ladder.

Stop Sounding Like Every Other Nonprofit

The original copy had every phrase you've ever read on a charity site. "Help students." "Support our mission." "Your gift makes a difference." None of it is wrong. None of it makes anyone click Donate.

A $50 donor and a $250 donor are different humans. They want different things. They give for different reasons. Writing the same line for both means you miss both.

The Fix: Different Pitch Per Tier

For the $50 tier I made it concrete. "Skip the admin. The exact materials the kid needs, in the kid's hands." Grassroots donors fear waste. Tell them their money goes directly into the thing.

For the $250 tier I shifted to legacy. "You're not paying for a bus ride. You're paying for the moment a student realizes the door is open." That donor wants to be the reason something happened. Sell them the outcome, not the line item.

Same school, same mission, two completely different conversations. Because they're two completely different people.

The Checkout That Killed the Vibe

This was the leak. They used Square for payments. Fine, Square works. But clicking "Donate" opened a new tab and dropped the donor on a generic, white-labeled Square checkout that looked nothing like the site they were just on.

That's the moment you lose them. They came in trusting a sharp, intentional brand. Now they're staring at what looks like a phishing page. Their brain says "wait, am I in the right place?" and the answer is "I'll come back later." They don't come back later.

The Fix: Bring the Checkout Onto the Page

I couldn't change the processor (the client wanted to keep Square). So I wrote a small piece of JavaScript that catches the click, kills the new tab, and pops the Square checkout in a window that hovers over the site they're already on. Same processor. Same security. Totally different experience.

// Catch the "Donate" button click and open the // Square checkout in a modal instead of a new tab. const buttons = document.querySelectorAll('.square-checkout-btn'); buttons.forEach(function(btn) { btn.addEventListener('click', function(e) { e.preventDefault(); const url = this.getAttribute('data-url'); window.open(url, 'Payment', 'width=500,height=720,top=100,left=400'); }); });

The vibe holds. The donor clicks a sharp custom button and a Square checkout floats over the page they were already on. No redirect. No broken trust. Donation processed.

The Real Lesson

None of this was hard. A new button. Two different paragraphs. A few lines of JavaScript. The original page wasn't broken because the designer was bad. It was broken because nobody asked "what does the donor's brain actually do on this page?"

That's the gap. Most websites have a beautiful version of the thing. Almost none of them have a converting version. Pretending those are the same thing costs you money every day the site is live.

If your site looks great but the leads aren't coming through, the design isn't the problem. The mechanics are. That's what I fix.
