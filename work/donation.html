CASE 01 // DONATION PAGE REBUILD RESULT: MORE DONATIONS, SAME TRAFFIC

A Pretty Page
That Didn't Make Money.

The page looked incredible. Dark, sharp, and brutalist. But donors still had to make a hard jump between giving levels, read the same pitch at every amount, and leave the branded site for a generic checkout. I changed those three parts.

The $50 to $250 Cliff

Their donation page had two options. Fifty bucks or two hundred fifty. Nothing in between. That's a 5x jump with no place to land.

For a donor who wanted to give more than $50, the next choice was $250. That five-times jump made the decision harder than it needed to be, so some donors left without choosing either amount.

The page made a willing donor work too hard to choose an amount.

The Fix: A $100 Bridge

I added a $100 tier. It gave people a natural middle choice and made $250 feel like the next step instead of a five-times jump.

  • $50: Buys the materials a student uses.
  • $100: Funds a local project from start to finish.
  • $250: Changes the trajectory of one kid's year.

Same page, one new button. Donations went up across every tier. The middle option pulled people up the ladder.

Stop Sounding Like Every Other Nonprofit

The original copy used familiar charity phrases: “Help students.” “Support our mission.” “Your gift makes a difference.” These broad lines gave donors no concrete reason to choose one amount over another.

The $50 donor wanted to know the money would reach something tangible. The $250 donor wanted to see the larger moment the gift could create. Each tier needed its own reason to give.

The Fix: Different Pitch Per Tier

For the $50 tier I made it concrete. "Skip the admin. The exact materials the kid needs, in the kid's hands." Grassroots donors fear waste. Tell them their money goes directly into the thing.

For the $250 tier I shifted to legacy. “A $250 gift gives a student the moment they realize the door is open.” That donor wants to be the reason something happened, so the copy shows the moment their money creates.

Each amount spoke to the reason that donor would choose it.

The Checkout That Killed the Vibe

This was the leak. They used Square for payments. Fine, Square works. But clicking "Donate" opened a new tab and dropped the donor on a generic, white-labeled Square checkout that looked nothing like the site they were just on.

The sharp, intentional site had earned trust. The generic checkout looked disconnected and made donors stop to question whether they were still in the right place.

The Fix: Bring the Checkout Onto the Page

I couldn't change the processor because the client wanted to keep Square. I wrote a small piece of JavaScript that opened the same secure Square checkout over the branded page instead of sending the donor to a new tab.

// Catch the "Donate" button click and open the // Square checkout in a modal instead of a new tab. const buttons = document.querySelectorAll('.square-checkout-btn'); buttons.forEach(function(btn) { btn.addEventListener('click', function(e) { e.preventDefault(); const url = this.getAttribute('data-url'); window.open(url, 'Payment', 'width=500,height=720,top=100,left=400'); }); });

The donor stayed on the branded page while the secure Square checkout opened above it. The payment processor stayed the same, and the transition felt connected to the site.

What the small repair changed

The repair was small: one new amount, copy written for each donor level, and a few lines of JavaScript. Each change removed one place where the donor had to stop and think.

Most websites are designed for appearance first. Conversion improves when the page also helps the visitor choose, act, and finish.

A site can look good and still lose buyers at the decision or checkout step. I fix those mechanics.
